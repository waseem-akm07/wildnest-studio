# Purpose

The WildNest Research Standard (WRS) defines the official methodology for conducting research across WildNest Studio.

Its purpose is to ensure that every research document is:

- Evidence-based
- Repeatable
- Actionable
- Objective
- Well-documented
- Continuously improvable

This standard applies to all research performed by humans, AI systems, or a combination of both.

Research is considered a strategic capability of WildNest Studio and forms the foundation for creative, technical, and business decisions.

---

# Core Philosophy

Research exists to improve decision-making.

The objective is **not** to collect information.

The objective is to transform information into knowledge, knowledge into insights, and insights into better strategic decisions.

Writing is the final step of research—not the first.

---

# Research Principles

Every research effort must follow these principles.

## 1. Evidence Before Opinion

Conclusions should be supported by credible evidence whenever possible.

Assumptions must be clearly labeled.

---

## 2. Curiosity Before Confirmation

Research should challenge existing beliefs rather than search only for evidence that supports them.

Seek disconfirming evidence.

---

## 3. Patterns Before Examples

Individual examples are useful.

Repeatable patterns are valuable.

Always ask:

> "Does this observation appear consistently across multiple sources?"

---

## 4. Strategy Before Information

Every research finding should help answer:

> "How does this improve WildNest Studio?"

If the answer is unclear, the finding should be reconsidered.

---

## 5. Long-Term Thinking

Prioritize insights that remain useful for years.

Avoid building strategy around temporary trends unless the document explicitly focuses on trend analysis.

---

# Research Lifecycle

Every research project follows the same lifecycle.

## Phase 1 — Define the Question

Clearly define the problem.

Examples:

- Why do viewers emotionally connect with animated characters?
- Why do some animation channels outperform others?
- Which AI tools best support our production pipeline?

A research project without a clear question should not begin.

---

## Phase 2 — Define Scope

Specify:

- Objectives
- Boundaries
- Assumptions
- Constraints
- Expected deliverables

A clear scope prevents research from becoming unfocused.

---

## Phase 3 — Form Initial Hypotheses

Before collecting evidence, write down the team's current assumptions.

Example:

- Strong storytelling matters more than animation complexity.
- Recurring characters increase audience loyalty.

These hypotheses are starting points—not conclusions.

---

## Phase 4 — Collect Evidence

Gather information from diverse, high-quality sources.

Preferred sources include:

- Academic research
- Industry reports
- Books
- Official documentation
- Creator interviews
- Public datasets
- Reputable publications
- Conference talks

Avoid relying on a single source whenever possible.

---

## Phase 5 — Evaluate Evidence

Every significant finding should be evaluated using the following questions:

- Is the source credible?
- Is the information current?
- Is it supported elsewhere?
- Could bias exist?
- Does conflicting evidence exist?

Low-confidence evidence should be clearly marked.

---

## Phase 6 — Extract Patterns

Move beyond isolated facts.

Identify recurring themes.

Transform observations into principles.

Example:

Observation

↓

Pattern

↓

Underlying Principle

↓

WildNest Opportunity

---

## Phase 7 — Strategic Analysis

Every research project must answer:

- Why does this matter?
- What should WildNest do?
- What should WildNest avoid?
- What risks exist?
- What opportunities exist?

Research is incomplete until it influences strategy.

---

## Phase 8 — Decision Support

If research affects future decisions, include clear recommendations.

Recommendations should be:

- Actionable
- Prioritized
- Supported by evidence
- Explicit about trade-offs

---

## Phase 9 — Documentation

Only after completing the previous phases should the final document be written.

Documentation captures the research process and conclusions.

It does not replace the research itself.

---

## Phase 10 — Review & Revision

Research is never considered permanently complete.

Every major document should be reviewed when:

- New evidence becomes available.
- Technology changes significantly.
- Industry conditions change.
- Major assumptions are challenged.

# Source Quality Framework

WildNest classifies sources using four confidence levels.

| Level | Description       | Typical Sources                                                         |
| ----- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A     | High Confidence   | Academic papers, official documentation, peer-reviewed research         |
| B     | Strong Confidence | Books, respected industry reports, creator interviews, conference talks |
| C     | Medium Confidence | Professional blogs, market analysis, reputable media                    |
| D     | Low Confidence    | Opinions, social posts, unsupported claims                              |

Whenever possible, strategic decisions should rely primarily on Level A and Level B sources.

---

# Research Deliverables

Every major research document should include:

- Executive Summary
- Research Question
- Objectives
- Methodology
- Findings
- Pattern Analysis
- Strategic Implications
- WildNest Takeaways
- Things We Should Adopt
- Things We Should Avoid
- CEO Recommendations
- Future Research
- References

---

# Documentation Standards

Every research document must include metadata.

Example:

- Document ID
- Version
- Status
- Owner
- Last Updated

Documents should use:

- Clear headings
- Tables where appropriate
- Evidence before conclusions
- Consistent terminology
- Cross-references to related documents

---

# Bias Checklist

Before finalizing any research, ask:

- Are we only looking for evidence that supports our idea?
- Have we considered opposing viewpoints?
- Have we mistaken correlation for causation?
- Are we relying too heavily on one successful example?
- Have we separated facts from opinions?
- Have we documented uncertainty?

---

# Decision Framework

Every major research project should conclude by answering:

1. What did we learn?
2. What surprised us?
3. What assumptions changed?
4. What should WildNest do next?
5. What risks remain?
6. What further research is required?

---

# Research Quality Checklist

Before a document is considered complete, verify:

- Research question is clearly defined.
- Scope is documented.
- Evidence is cited.
- Assumptions are identified.
- Patterns are extracted.
- Strategic implications are explained.
- Recommendations are actionable.
- References are included.
- Document follows WRS.

---

# Continuous Improvement

The WildNest Research Standard is a living document.

It should evolve as:

- Better research methods emerge.
- New AI capabilities become available.
- WildNest Studio gains practical experience.
- Industry best practices improve.

Improving the research process is itself considered an ongoing research activity.

---

# Final Principle

> **Research is successful only when it leads to better decisions.**

Collecting information is not the goal.

Building enduring knowledge that improves WildNest Studio is the goal.
