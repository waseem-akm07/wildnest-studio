# Story Framework

> How to structure episodes, write comedy, and maintain story quality.

---

## Content Formats

| Format | Duration | Use |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Micro Short** | 15 seconds | Viral hooks, single-gag loops |
| **Short** | 30 seconds | Character intros, signature gag showcase |
| **Mini Episode** | 60 seconds | Full mini-story with emotional arc |
| **Standard Episode** | 2.5 minutes | Core episodic content (Season 1 format) |
| **Extended Episode** | 8 minutes | Deep character development, A+B plots |
| **Seasonal Special** | 12 minutes | Holiday events, season finales |

---

## The 6-Beat Framework

Every WildNest story follows this structure:

```
BEAT 1: THE HOOK  (0–5%)    ──► Establish normal life + sudden trigger
BEAT 2: THE BEND  (5–25%)   ──► First attempt + complication begins
BEAT 3: THE TWIST (25–50%)  ──► Major setback escalates out of control
BEAT 4: THE CHAOS (50–85%)  ──► Climax: extreme slapstick + 1.5s reaction holds
BEAT 5: THE RESET (85–95%)  ──► Teamwork resolution before deadline
BEAT 6: THE LOOP  (95–100%) ──► Final button joke + seamless loop
```

### Beat Timing by Format

| Beat | 30s Short | 60s Short | 2.5min Episode | 8min Episode |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **1. Hook** | 0–3s | 0–5s | 0–15s | 0–40s |
| **2. Bend** | 3–8s | 5–15s | 15–40s | 40–120s |
| **3. Twist** | 8–15s | 15–30s | 40–75s | 120–240s |
| **4. Chaos** | 15–24s | 30–50s | 75–125s | 240–410s |
| **5. Reset** | 24–28s | 50–56s | 125–142s | 410–460s |
| **6. Loop** | 28–30s | 56–60s | 142–150s | 460–480s |

## Story Types

Here are the 12 types of stories you can write for Critter Haven Resort:

1. **Workplace Resort Comedy** — Staff running operations under deadline pressure
2. **Slice of Life Cozy** — Seed tasting, napping, quiet friendship moments
3. **Micro-Scale Adventure** — Exploring new house territories (attic, garden, under-bed)
4. **Scavenged Item Mystery** — Investigating a strange new human object (vibrating alarm clock?)
5. **Critter Competition** — Hamster wheel races, cotton-stacking contests
6. **Seasonal/Holiday Special** — Winter heating vent festivals, Halloween candy raids
7. **Emotional Sincerity Beat** — Fear of abandonment, rebuilding trust, helping a lonely guest
8. **Improvisational Teamwork** — Overcoming a disaster by combining unique character skills
9. **Resort Guest Rescue** — Rescuing a clumsy guest stuck in a tube
10. **The Misunderstanding** — Barnaby misinterprets a simple situation as a catastrophe
11. **Invention Gone Wrong** — Pip's contraption backfires hilariously
12. **Everyday Critter Struggle** — Trying to carry a seed that's too big for your mouth

## Comedy Rules

Humor is driven by **Visual Benign Violation (BVT):**

1. **Benign Violation** — Extreme physical shock disarmed by soft cartoon physics & cute squeaks
2. **1.5s Reaction Holds** — Freeze keyframes for 36 frames with pin-prick pupils before the punchline
3. **Rule of Three** — Establish pattern twice, subvert wildly on the third time
4. **Ego vs Reality** — Character's pride collapsing into wide-eyed clumsy panic
5. **Visual Callbacks** — Reintroduce a minor prop from Beat 1 as the climax trigger

> **The Mute Test:** If a joke requires dialogue to be funny, it's cut. Physical performance leads 100% of punchlines.

---

## Emotional Rules

Comedy opens the heart; sincerity cements attachment:

- **Vulnerability:** Characters must show clear emotional vulnerabilities
- **Pratfall Empathy:** Seeing a proud character make an embarrassing mistake makes them relatable
- **The Sincerity Beat:** Every episode needs at least one 10-second moment of genuine warmth without a joke interrupt
- **Small Victories:** Celebrate small wins (fixing a tube leak, sharing a sunflower seed)

---

## Story Rules (Never Break These)

1. **Clear Initial Goal** — Character must want something specific within the first 15 seconds
2. **No Deus Ex Machina** — Problems solved through character choices and teamwork, never lucky coincidence
3. **The 5:00 PM Clock** — Deadline pressure must actively constrain every crisis
4. **The Mute Test** — 80% of narrative meaning must work with audio at zero
5. **Respect the Reset** — Physical world returns to order; character flaws reset for next episode

---

## Character Usage Rules

- **Barnaby** drives or influences 70%+ of main series episodes
- Always pair characters with **opposing personality traits** for friction
- Every secondary character must have a distinct job and comedic function
- Visiting guests are the **catalyst** for staff's emotional growth or comedy

---

## Running Gags

Manage gags with the **Rule-of-3 Lifecycle:**

```
Stage 1: INTRODUCTION (Ep 1–3)   ──► Establish the gag clearly
Stage 2: EVOLUTION (Ep 4–15)     ──► Vary context, subvert expectations
Stage 3: CALLBACK / RETIRE       ──► Use sparingly for nostalgic payoff; retire before fatigue
```

**Core running gags:**
- Barnaby's bowtie adjust when nervous
- Cheek-stuffing inflation (head swells 3x)
- The 1.5s reaction freeze with pin-prick pupils

## Season Planning

```
Season Arc (24 Episodes) = 80% Standalone Episodes + 20% World Expansion Arc
```

- **Season 1:** Establishing the resort, core staff friction (Barnaby & Pip)
- **Season 3:** Unlocking Closet Wing and Attic Waterpark, regional guests
- **Season 5:** Backyard sky-tubes, outdoor squirrel rivalries
- **Finale formula:** Double the physical stakes + reaffirm staff loyalty

---

## Script Quality Checklist

Before writing a script, make sure it passes these checks:

- [ ] Follows the 6-Beat structure?
- [ ] Character's goal is clear within 15 seconds?
- [ ] Passes the Silent Mute Test (understandable without audio)?
- [ ] Physical slapstick uses BVT physics and 1.5s reaction holds?
- [ ] Dialogue follows Hinglish rules (max 8 words per line)?
- [ ] Crisis is caused by a character's flaw, not random luck?
- [ ] Resolution comes from character choices, not coincidence?
- [ ] Has a 10-second sincerity beat (genuine warmth)?
- [ ] The 5:00 PM ticking clock is active?
- [ ] Has visual details that reward rewatching?
